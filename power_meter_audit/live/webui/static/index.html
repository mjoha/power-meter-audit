    Protocol

    Every power step is ridden at two cadences. At a fixed power, 70 rpm loads the cranks about 29% harder than 90 rpm, so each step gives two crank torques — that is what separates a torque-dependent error from a power-dependent one.

      Consistency is the verdict that needs no assumptions. A left-only pedal read against a direct-drive trainer should sit a few percent high from drivetrain loss and imbalance, and neither of those varies with power or cadence — so a flat ratio means the two are aligned whatever its value, while a ratio that drifts across the grid points at a real fault.
