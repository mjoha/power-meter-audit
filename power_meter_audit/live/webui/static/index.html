    Protocol

    Every power step is ridden at two cadences. At a fixed power, 70 rpm loads the cranks about 29% harder than 90 rpm, so each step gives two crank torques — that is what separates a torque-dependent error from a power-dependent one.

        Result

        Consistency— spread across the grid
        Offset— mean ratio vs expected band
        Fit— pedals against trainer

        Consistency asks whether the pedals/trainer ratio stays the same across every power × cadence cell. Green means it is flat. A left-only pedal should read a few percent high (drivetrain loss plus left/right imbalance), but those do not change with watts or rpm — so a ratio that moves across the grid cannot be explained by either, and is the verdict that actually matters.

        Offset is how high or low the pedals sit versus the trainer, averaged over usable cells. For left-only pedals against a direct-drive, about +2% to +9% is expected. Green here only means that average is in-band; it does not mean the two meters agree in watts, and it is secondary to consistency.

        Fit is the straight line pedals ≈ slope × trainer + intercept. Slope near 1 with a small intercept is a constant offset. A slope away from 1, or a large intercept, means the gap itself changes with power.

        In zone
        Share of the measurement window where trainer cadence stayed within the protocol tolerance of the target. Below 70% the cell is excluded, so a sloppy cadence cannot poison the ratio. Cadence comes from the trainer, not the pedals, so a pedal cadence error cannot cherry-pick the samples.
        Samples
        Trainer / pedal power readings kept after that cadence filter. At least 15 of each are required before the cell counts. Pedals report once per crank revolution, so this number is usually smaller than the trainer’s ~1 Hz stream.
